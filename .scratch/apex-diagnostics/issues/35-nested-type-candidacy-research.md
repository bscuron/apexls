## Question

Investigate whether/how to extend this map's diagnostic candidacy to whole nested/inner types (`Class`/`Interface`/`Enum` declared inside another type) -- currently structurally, cleanly excluded from both `visibility_narrowing_diagnostics` (`is_narrowing_candidate_kind`, `crates/apex-binder/src/visibility_narrowing.rs:54-64`) and `dead_code_diagnostics` (`is_dead_code_candidate_kind`, `crates/apex-binder/src/dead_code.rs:77-88`), with zero partial work either place. Two related, previously-unaddressed gaps, folded into one research ticket at the user's explicit direction since both are "nested-type candidacy" gaps discovered together and may share real design/architecture:

1. **Nested-type visibility narrowing**: could a nested type's own declared visibility be narrowed the same way `visibility_narrowing_diagnostics` already narrows member visibility?
2. **Nested-type dead-code detection**: could a nested type that's itself never referenced anywhere be flagged the way `dead_code_diagnostics` already flags an unused member?

Prior survey already established (do not re-investigate, use as given):

- This binder accepts **any** visibility keyword uniformly on a nested type today (`ModifierSet::from_modifiers`, `crates/apex-binder/src/symbol.rs:123-149`, called identically for `ClassDecl`/`InterfaceDecl`/`EnumDecl` and for members) -- no kind-specific validation exists anywhere in the parser/binder.
- `SymbolTable::is_visible_from` (`crates/apex-binder/src/symbol_table.rs:461-490`) only ever inspects a *member's own* visibility -- it never reads a container type's own `Visibility` at all, and neither does type-reference resolution (`resolve_type_ref_base`/`nested_type_visible_from`, `crates/apex-binder/src/resolve.rs:1104-1117` and `symbol_table.rs:598-611`). So today, in this binder's model, a `public` member on a `private` nested class resolves as visible from anywhere -- nothing caps it.
- Real NPSP corpus (rough, line-anchored grep, not binder-driven): 684 nested-type declarations with an explicit visibility keyword -- 499 `public`, 179 `private`, 0 `protected`, 6 `global`. A real but meaningfully smaller opportunity than the member-level check's ~5,752 raw candidates.

Establish, concretely:

- **What visibility keywords are actually legal on a nested type in real, compiled Apex** -- `public`/`private` only, or also `protected`/`global`? This binder's own permissiveness proves nothing (see above); settle it via the `sf` CLI against a connected org (this project's established oracle practice, `apex-grammar-oracle-sf-cli`), the same way ticket 02 settled duplicate/conflicting-modifier rules. The zero-`protected`-nested-types-in-NPSP signal is usage evidence only, not proof of illegality -- confirm directly.
- **Does real Apex cap a member's *effective* reachability by its container type's own visibility** -- i.e., is a `public` method on a `private` nested class actually unreachable from outside the file in real compiled Apex, even though the method itself is declared `public`? This is the map's own "blast radius" concern from its Not-yet-specified note, and it's a genuine unresolved semantics question, not something this binder's model already encodes one way or the other (confirmed above: `is_visible_from` doesn't check container visibility at all today). If real Apex does cap it, narrowing a type's visibility changes what its members' *individually* narrower-or-required visibility even means, and this diagnostic's own member-level `required_visibility` computation may need to account for it. If real Apex does *not* cap it (each declaration's own visibility is independently authoritative, container visibility notwithstanding), then "narrow the type" is a much simpler, more isolated suggestion with no interaction with the member-level check at all. Settle via the `sf` CLI: a real deploy test with a `private` nested class holding a `public` method, referenced (or attempted to be referenced) from an unrelated class in another file.
- **Whether the existing member-level machinery generalizes cleanly** -- `declaring_type_of_reference`, `top_level_container`, `subtypes`/`inherited_chain` were all built for member candidates; sketch concretely whether a type-level `required_visibility` computation (bucketing *references to the type itself*, e.g. via `new Outer.Inner()`, `Outer.Inner` type references, `instanceof`, etc. -- not references to its members) can reuse these as-is, or needs new binder-level plumbing (e.g. does `ReferenceTable`/`references_to` even record a reference *to a type symbol itself* today, the way it records references to methods/fields? Check concretely, don't assume).
- **Nested-type dead-code detection's own real shape**: does `dead_code.rs`'s existing `references_to`/`references_to_in_file` split (file-scoped for `Private`, project-wide for `Public`) apply the same way to a type reference as it does to a member reference? Any exemption channel (annotation-driven platform invocation, Visualforce-page reference) that already matters for members -- does any of it apply differently, or at all, to a whole type (e.g. can a Visualforce page name a *nested* class as its controller/extension, or only a top-level one)?
- **Real NPSP corpus signal for the dead-code side specifically**: of the 684 nested types found, roughly how many appear to have zero real references at all (a rough scan is fine, mirroring ticket 32's own precedent) -- sizes the dead-code opportunity independently from the narrowing one, since they may turn out to be very differently sized.
- **Whether these two gaps (narrowing, dead-code) should ship as one combined ticket chain or split into two independent decision/implement chains once their own architecture is clearer** -- do not decide this now, just note what the research itself suggests once the above is answered; the follow-on decision ticket(s) get charted once this ticket resolves, per this map's own established "discovered from how tickets actually end up blocking each other, not asserted up front" practice.

This ticket is fact-finding only -- it does not decide any architecture. Its findings feed a follow-on decision ticket (or two).

## Answer

### 1. Legal visibility keywords on a nested type, and whether real Apex caps a member's effective reachability by its container's own visibility

Verified against the same connected org ticket 02/32 used (`bscuron19.f9f273f0b808@agentforce.com`, confirmed connected via `sf org list`, `instanceApiVersion: "67.0"`), following the identical methodology: a scratch SFDX project outside the repo checkout, one or a few throwaway Apex classes deployed at a time via `sf project deploy start --source-dir ... -o org --json`, cleaned up afterward via `sf project delete source --metadata "ApexClass:..." -o org --no-prompt --json`. Every deploy that actually created a component in the org was deleted afterward (confirmed via `sf project delete source`'s own `deletedSource` response, then a follow-up `SELECT Name FROM ApexClass WHERE Name LIKE 'NT35Probe%'` returning zero rows); every deploy that failed (`numberComponentsDeployed: 0`) needed no cleanup, matching ticket 02's own finding for that case.

**`protected` on a nested class: illegal, rejected at deploy time.** `public class NT35ProbeA { protected class NestedProtected { public Integer x() { return 1; } } }` failed with `"protected is not allowed on classes (2:21)"` -- a real, compiler-enforced rule, not just a usage convention. This upgrades the prior survey's "0 `protected` nested types in the real NPSP corpus" from usage evidence to confirmed illegality: there is no legal way to write one at all, so a `protected` nested type is not just rare, it's impossible.

**`global` on a nested class: legal, but only when the *enclosing* type is itself `global`.** `global class NT35ProbeA { global class NestedGlobal { public Integer x() { return 1; } } }` deployed successfully (`numberComponentsDeployed: 1`, cleaned up after). But `public class NT35ProbeB { global class NestedGlobalInPublicOuter { ... } }` failed with `"Enclosing type for global classes in apex classes must be declared as global (1:14)"`. So `global` is a real, legal nested-type visibility keyword -- but it carries a container-visibility precondition member-level visibility never has (a member's own visibility never constrains what its container is allowed to be).

**Does real Apex cap a member's effective reachability by its container's own visibility -- yes, but the mechanism is precise: it's a *declared-type-visibility* check at each point of use, not a blanket "members of a private class are unreachable" rule.** Three probes, all against one outer `NT35ProbeD` with a `private class SecretNested { public Integer secretValue() { return 42; } }` and `public static SecretNested makeSecretNested() { return new SecretNested(); }`:

1. **Naming the private nested type directly from an unrelated file** (`NT35ProbeD.SecretNested x = NT35ProbeD.makeSecretNested();` in `NT35ProbeF`, a separate class): **fails**, twice over (the declaration and the call) -- `"Type is not visible: NT35ProbeD.SecretNested"`. A private nested type's own name is genuinely not visible outside its declaring file's type family, exactly mirroring the member-level `private` rule.
2. **Reaching the public method indirectly, through the accessor, without ever naming the private type** (`NT35ProbeD.makeSecretNested().secretValue()` in `NT35ProbeE`, a separate class, using the accessor's own inferred return type rather than spelling it out): **still fails** -- `"Method return type NT35ProbeD.SecretNested is not visible for: NT35ProbeD.SecretNested NT35ProbeD.makeSecretNested()"`. Note the error names the *return type*, not the method itself, as not visible -- `makeSecretNested`'s own `public` visibility is never in question; its *declared return type* is what the compiler refuses to expose across the visibility boundary. So yes: a `public` method's own visibility keyword is not, by itself, sufficient to make it reachable -- if the type it's declared to operate on (here, its own return type) isn't itself visible at the call site, the call is rejected regardless of the member's own visibility.
3. **Reaching the same private nested class's method through a *publicly-visible* declared type instead** (`SecretNested implements NT35ProbeGInterface` [a `public interface { Integer secretValue(); }`], accessor changed to `public static NT35ProbeGInterface makeSecretNested() { return new SecretNested(); }`, called identically from an unrelated `NT35ProbeH` via `NT35ProbeD.makeSecretNested().secretValue()`): **compiles and deploys successfully.** The private nested class is still never named anywhere outside `NT35ProbeD`; the only thing `NT35ProbeH` ever touches is the public interface type. This confirms the cap is specifically on any *reference to the private type's own name/declared-type-slot* leaking outside its family -- not on "an instance whose runtime class happens to be a private nested type can never have its members called from outside." Dispatched through a publicly-visible declared type (an interface, and by the same reasoning very likely a public base class, though only the interface case was directly tested), the member is fully, ordinarily reachable.

**Concrete takeaway for the follow-on design ticket**: narrowing a nested type's own visibility is a real, independent, potentially breaking change distinct from narrowing any of its members -- narrowing the *type* can turn a currently-legal external reference (case 1/2 above) into a compile error, even where every individual member's own visibility stays untouched. This is exactly the "blast radius" concern the map's own Not-yet-specified note flagged, now confirmed real rather than hypothetical. It also means a type-level narrowing diagnostic's own reference-bucketing (§3) has to look at references *to the type itself* (declared-type slots, `new` targets, `instanceof`, `extends`/`implements`), not member call sites, to decide safety -- confirmed both possible and necessary by §2/§3 below.

### 2. Does `ReferenceTable`/`references_to` record a reference to a type symbol itself

**Yes, confirmed concretely by reading every real call site, not inferred from `Resolution`'s shape.** `resolve_type_ref_base` (`crates/apex-binder/src/resolve.rs:1046-1203`) is the one function backing every kind of `Type` reference in this binder (its own doc comment, `resolve.rs:1000-1016`: "a field/param/local/return type, a `new`/`instanceof`/cast target, an `extends`/`implements` supertype, ..."), and it records `refs.set(ptr, Resolution::Resolved(id))` -- the *type's own* `SymbolId` -- at every resolving branch that finds a project-local type:

- the bare-unqualified-nested-type case, `resolve.rs:1104-1109` (`table.nested_type_visible_from(container, &name)` succeeds -> `refs.set(ptr, Resolution::Resolved(id))`);
- the dotted-top-level case, `resolve.rs:1114-1117` (`resolve_dotted_top_level` succeeds -> same `Resolution::Resolved(id)`).

For a qualified `Outer.Inner` reference, `record_qualified_segments` (`resolve.rs:1290-1368`) *additionally* records each dotted segment's own prefix resolution (`Outer` -> `Outer`'s `SymbolId`, `Outer.Inner` -> `Inner`'s `SymbolId`) keyed by that segment's own token (`resolve.rs:1321`, `1354`, `1363-1366`), all still `Resolution::Resolved(id)`.

`Resolution::symbol_ids` (`reference_table.rs:230-241`) returns `std::slice::from_ref(id)` for `Resolution::Resolved(id)` -- exactly the same extraction every method/field/property reference goes through -- and `ReferenceTable::set` (`reference_table.rs:346-354`) feeds that straight into `by_symbol: FxHashMap<SymbolId, Vec<SyntaxPtr>>` (`reference_table.rs:309`), the identical reverse index `references_to` reads. There is **no separate code path, no different `Resolution` variant, and no special-casing** for a type-symbol reference versus a member reference anywhere in this pipeline -- it's the exact same mechanism, confirmed by direct code reading, not assumed from `Resolution`'s enum shape.

Every shape the ticket asked about routes through this one function:
- **`new Outer.Inner()`**: `bind_new_expr` (`resolve.rs:3627-3711`) calls `self.resolve_type_ref(&t)` at `resolve.rs:3628` for the `new` target -- so a `new` expression records **two separate reference entries**: one against `Inner`'s own type `SymbolId` (via `resolve_type_ref`, keyed by the `Type` node's own `SyntaxPtr`), and a second, independent one against whichever `Constructor` `SymbolId` the arguments narrow to (`resolve.rs:3672-3687`, `self.refs.set_with_highlight(ptr, highlight, resolution)`, keyed by the `NewExpr` node's own `SyntaxPtr` with a narrowed highlight range). `references_to(inner_type_id)` picks up the first; `references_to(constructor_id)` picks up the second -- they don't collide or double-count.
- **A bare/qualified `Outer.Inner`-typed field/param/local/return-type declaration**: goes straight through `resolve_type_ref`/`resolve_type_ref_base` with no body to walk (`bind_type_ref`, `resolve.rs:1375-1392`), used identically for declaration-site types.
- **`instanceof`**: `Expr::Instanceof(i) => { ...; if let Some(t) = i.type_ref() { self.resolve_type_ref(&t); } }` (`resolve.rs:2280-2285`) -- same function, same recording.
- **`extends`/`implements` supertypes**: bound as their own Pass-2 stage, `supertype_bound` (`lib.rs:676-705`), which calls `resolve::bind_type_ref` at `lib.rs:692` for every `(owner, ptr)` in `cache.supertype_ptrs` -- the exact same `resolve_type_ref` path, not a separate inheritance-only resolution mechanism.

`BoundProgram::references_to`/`references_to_in_file` (`lib.rs:1080-1084`, `1086-1098`) are themselves fully generic over `SymbolId` -- neither has any kind-awareness (`Method` vs. `Class` vs. anything else) anywhere in their implementation, so no change is needed there at all for a type-level query to work.

### 3. Whether the existing member-level narrowing machinery generalizes

**`declaring_type_of_reference` and `top_level_container` generalize with zero changes -- confirmed by reading their bodies, neither one inspects the *candidate*'s own kind at all.**

- `declaring_type_of_reference(program: &BoundProgram, reference: SyntaxPtr) -> Option<SymbolId>` (`visibility_narrowing.rs:131-156`) only ever looks at the *reference site*'s own ancestor chain (`.ancestors().find(...)` for the nearest `ClassDecl`/`InterfaceDecl`/`EnumDecl`) -- it has no parameter or logic that varies by what kind of symbol is being bucketed. Directly reusable, unmodified, for a type-level candidate.
- `top_level_container(program: &BoundProgram, id: SymbolId) -> SymbolId` (`visibility_narrowing.rs:71-77`) is a plain `container` walk over any `SymbolId` -- a nested type's own `container` field (`Symbol::container`, `symbol.rs:200`) is populated exactly the same way a member's is. Directly reusable, unmodified.

**`required_visibility`'s own body is generically written (nothing kind-specific in its logic), but is *not* safely reusable as-is -- it can suggest an illegal state for a type candidate.** `required_visibility` (`visibility_narrowing.rs:181-215`) is parameterized purely over `id`/`declaring_top_level`/`subtype_set`/`current`, with no kind check anywhere in its body -- structurally, it would run fine against a `Class`/`Interface`/`Enum` `id` exactly as written. The problem is its own `Public` branch: when a reference is reachable only via inheritance (`subtype_set.contains(&from)`, line 197), it sets `needs_protected = true`, and the function's final match (lines 206-213) then returns `Some(Visibility::Protected)` for that case (line 208-209). **§1 just confirmed `protected` is flatly illegal on a nested type** ("protected is not allowed on classes") -- so reusing `required_visibility` verbatim against a type candidate could compute and suggest `Visibility::Protected` as the "narrower required visibility" for a `Class`/`Interface`/`Enum`, which is not a state that type could legally be declared in at all. This is a real, concrete correctness gap the follow-on design ticket needs to close, not a hypothetical: the type-level version needs its own three-way disjunction that collapses the `needs_protected` case to "stays `Public`, not narrowable" (there's no intermediate legal state a nested type can occupy between `Public` and `Private` the way a member can occupy `Protected`), rather than reusing this function's `Public` branch unmodified.

**New surface a type-level version would need, concretely:**
- A type-level `is_narrowing_candidate_kind` equivalent: `matches!(symbol.kind, SymbolKind::Class | SymbolKind::Interface | SymbolKind::Enum) && symbol.container.is_some() && symbol.modifiers.visibility == Visibility::Public` -- deliberately **not** `Public | Protected` the way the member-level check is (`visibility_narrowing.rs:58-61`): `Protected` is never a real starting state for a nested type at all (§1), so there's nothing to even check for it, unlike the member-level check where `Protected` is a real, legal, reachable starting visibility.
- No obvious type-level analogue of `overrides_or_implements` (`visibility_narrowing.rs:95-113`): Apex's "can't narrow below an override/interface contract" rule is specific to methods overriding/implementing other methods. This ticket did not independently verify whether real Apex places any equivalent restriction on a subclass's own declared visibility relative to its superclass's (e.g., can a `private` nested class `extend` a `public` one?) -- **flagged as unresolved**, worth a quick follow-up check only if the design ticket's scope actually needs it.
- `Global`'s own extra container-visibility precondition (§1: a nested `global` type requires a `global` *outer*) is a genuinely new consideration member-level narrowing never had -- a member's own (potentially narrower) visibility never constrained what its container was allowed to be. Since `Global` is already out of scope for narrowing generally (ticket 33's own settled scope, `visibility_narrowing.rs:12-15`), this mainly matters if `Global` narrowing is ever brought into scope later.

### 4. Nested-type dead-code detection's own real shape

`is_dead_code_candidate_kind` (`dead_code.rs:77-88`) would need a type-level counterpart: `matches!(symbol.kind, Class | Interface | Enum) && symbol.container.is_some() && matches!(visibility, Public | Private)` -- mirroring §3's finding that only `Public`/`Private` are even legal starting states for a nested type (`Protected` illegal, `Global` already excluded by `dead_code.rs`'s own existing "unprovable by local analysis regardless" reasoning, `dead_code.rs:36-40`, independent of the new `global`-requires-`global`-outer constraint from §1).

**`references_to`/`references_to_in_file` split: reuses unchanged.** Both are fully generic over `SymbolId` (§2, `lib.rs:1080-1098`) -- a `Private` nested type's dead-code check would use `references_to_in_file` exactly like a `Private` member does today, and a `Public` one `references_to`. Ticket 32's own §2 finding -- that `private` is genuinely scoped to the *whole file's type family*, not the literal declaring class, confirmed empirically against a real org -- applies identically here: a nested type's own `private` visibility is file-scoped the same way a `private` member's is, so `references_to_in_file`'s existing file-scoping is already the *correct* lookup for a type-level `Private` candidate too, no new empirical check needed.

**`has_platform_invocation_annotation`: does not generalize as-is; needs new (but cheap) logic.** `PLATFORM_INVOCATION_ANNOTATIONS` (`dead_code.rs:65-75`) is a member-only annotation list (`@AuraEnabled`, `@InvocableMethod`, `@InvocableVariable`, `@RemoteAction`, `@Http*`) -- every one of these annotates a *method/property*, never a class declaration itself, and `annotations_of` (`dead_code.rs:129-163`) explicitly returns an empty `Vec` for anything other than `Method`/`Property`/`Constructor`/`Field` (`dead_code.rs:134-139`), so a nested type can never itself carry one. A type-level exemption channel needs different logic: "does this type directly declare (`members_of(id)`) any member carrying one of these annotations" -- since the platform reaches such a member (Flow/Aura/REST) without ever needing a textual Apex reference to the *type* either, the same way it reaches the member with no reference to the member itself. Cheap to add (one `members_of` iteration reusing the existing per-member `has_platform_invocation_annotation` check), but genuinely new code, not a reuse.

**`is_visualforce_referenced`: only ever proves the *top-level* class's own name, never a nested type's own name -- and empirically, no real VF page ever names a nested class at all.** `is_visualforce_referenced` (`dead_code.rs:229-236`) already walks to `top_level_container(program, container)` regardless of how deeply `symbol` is nested, so a **member** inside a nested class is already correctly exempted today whenever the *outer* class is VF-referenced (nesting depth doesn't matter for the existing member-level check). But this means the exemption is keyed entirely on the top-level name -- it structurally has no way to recognize "this VF page names *this nested type specifically*," and checking whether real VF pages ever do that at all: grepped every `controller`/`extensions` attribute value across all 80 real `.page` files in the NPSP corpus (`grep -rhoE '(controller|extensions)="[^"]*"' tests/corpus/npsp --include='*.page'`) -- every single one of the ~70 distinct values found is a bare, unqualified class name (e.g. `STG_PanelRel_CTRL`, `ADDR_Validator`); **zero** dotted (`Outer.Inner`) shapes anywhere. `apex_metadata::visualforce::controller_and_extension_classes` (`visualforce.rs:46-62`) itself has no dot-aware parsing at all -- it takes each attribute value (or comma-split segment) as a literal class name string, and `is_visualforce_referenced` matches it purely by lowercase-string-equality against `top_level_container`'s own name (`dead_code.rs:233-235`), with no path that would even correctly resolve a dotted nested-class name if one existed. **Not conclusively settled from official Salesforce documentation** (not checked here, and a metadata deploy of a `.page` file wouldn't validate this either way -- it would need an actual page render against a connected org's Visualforce runtime, out of this ticket's sf-CLI-deploy-only methodology) -- but the real corpus evidence plus this binder's own total absence of a dot-handling path both point the same way: **treat VF's `controller`/`extensions` attribute as top-level-class-only** for design purposes, and flag this specific sub-question as not 100% conclusively proven if it matters later.

**`is_platform_invoked_test_method`: structurally inapplicable to type-level candidacy, confirmed via `sf` CLI -- not "generalizes," genuinely doesn't apply at all.** This check keys off two things: the legacy `testmethod` modifier or an `@isTest`/`@TestSetup` annotation, and (implicitly, since it only fires for `SymbolKind::Method`) `static`. Checked directly: `public class NT35ProbeI { @isTest private class NestedTestClass { @isTest static void doTest() { System.assert(true); } } }` failed to deploy with **two** separate errors -- `"IsTest can only be used on a top level type (3:19)"` and `"static can only be used on methods of a top level type (5:21)"`. So a nested type can never itself be `@isTest`-annotated, and (independently reconfirming ticket 32's own finding) can never declare `static` members at all. **A nested type can never be "the platform-invoked test class" the way a top-level one can** -- this whole exemption channel should simply not exist at the type level, not be generalized or ported.

### 5. Real NPSP corpus signal for the dead-code side

Ran a throwaway integration test (`crates/apex-binder/tests/ticket35_nested_type_reference_scan.rs`, deleted before this commit, per this project's ticket-11/ticket-32 precedent for this kind of fact-finding scan) against the real NPSP checkout at `tests/corpus/npsp`, pinned commit `3dc817c94e3f2d41d461f6a79dd9e83c279ded9c` (submodule freshly initialized for this ticket; hit the identical Windows `MAX_PATH` long-filename caveat ticket 32 already documented on a handful of unrelated metadata-translation/validation-rule files, but `git submodule status` showed the pin checked out clean and a direct count came back 1,044 `.cls` + 26 `.trigger` = 1,070 files, matching `tests/corpus/manifest.toml`'s own documented count and confirming the Apex source set itself is complete).

Using `apex_binder::BoundProgram::from_files` and `program.symbols.iter()` filtered to `SymbolKind::Class | Interface | Enum` with `container.is_some()`, cross-referenced against `program.references_to(id).next().is_none()`:

| kind | zero-reference | non-zero-reference | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Class` | 12 | 622 | 634 |
| `Interface` | 1 | 28 | 29 |
| `Enum` | 0 | 62 | 62 |
| **all nested types** | **13** | **712** | **725** |

(725 total nested types here counts every nested `Class`/`Interface`/`Enum` regardless of whether it carries an explicit visibility keyword; the prior survey's 684 count was explicit-visibility-only, so the ~41-symbol gap is nested types defaulting to `Private` with no keyword written -- not a discrepancy, a different filter.)

**The dead-code opportunity for nested types is real but small: 13/725 (~1.8%)** -- meaningfully smaller than both the member-level narrowing opportunity (ticket 32: ~5,752 real candidates) and, on its face, smaller than the type-level narrowing opportunity implied by the prior survey's 684 nested types with an explicit visibility keyword (499 `public` alone, before any reference-narrowing check runs). Also notable: 712/725 (~98%) of real nested types already have at least one recorded reference via the exact mechanism §2 confirmed -- concrete, corpus-scale confirmation that type-symbol reference recording isn't just theoretically wired up, it's actually populated for the overwhelming majority of real nested types today.

### 6. One combined chain or two independent chains

Per the ticket's own instruction, not decided here -- just what this research surfaces. The two gaps turned out to have meaningfully different real shapes once investigated:

- **Narrowing** needs genuinely new logic (a type-level candidate-kind filter that excludes `Protected` entirely, and a `required_visibility` variant that can't reuse the member-level function's `Protected`-suggesting branch), but reuses `declaring_type_of_reference`/`top_level_container` completely unchanged, and its own real candidate pool is comparatively large (684 nested types with an explicit visibility keyword, 499 of them already `Public`, before any reference-based narrowing check even runs).
- **Dead-code** reuses `references_to`/`references_to_in_file` and the private-file-scoping logic completely unchanged, needs new logic only for the platform-invocation-annotation channel, and needs to explicitly *drop* one whole exemption channel (`is_platform_invoked_test_method`) that structurally can never apply at the type level -- but its own real candidate pool (13/725 zero-reference nested types, §5) is roughly two orders of magnitude smaller than narrowing's.

Given how differently sized and differently shaped the two turned out to be -- narrowing carrying real cross-cutting correctness hazards (§1's reachability-capping finding, §3's illegal-`Protected`-suggestion hazard) that dead-code doesn't share at all, and a candidate pool nearly 100x dead-code's -- splitting into two independent decision/implement chains looks more likely to be the right call than shipping one combined ticket. Not asserted as the decision; left for the follow-on charting step.
